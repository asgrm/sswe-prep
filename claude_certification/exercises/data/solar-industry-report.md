## Page 12

Global solar photovoltaic capacity reached 1,865 GW by the end of 2024, an
increase of 29% year-over-year. China accounted for 43% of new installations,
followed by the United States at 12% and India at 9%.

Utility-scale installations represented 62% of new capacity, with distributed
rooftop systems making up the remaining 38%.

## Page 13

The global average levelized cost of electricity (LCOE) for utility-scale solar
fell to $38 per MWh in 2024, down from $44 per MWh in 2023. In the sunniest
regions (Middle East, Chile, Australia), winning auction bids came in below
$15 per MWh.

Module prices declined 34% during 2024 due to polysilicon oversupply, reaching
a historic low of $0.09 per watt for mainstream monocrystalline modules.

## Page 14

Commercial monocrystalline silicon panel efficiency has increased 25% in the
last decade: the market average moved from 17.8% (2014) to 22.3% (2024).
Laboratory perovskite-silicon tandem cells demonstrated 34.6% efficiency in
controlled conditions, though commercial availability is not expected before
2027.

Bifacial modules now account for 51% of the utility-scale market, offering
energy yield gains of 5-9% depending on ground albedo.

## Page 15

Grid integration remains the primary constraint on solar growth. Curtailment
rates in high-penetration markets reached 8.4% in California and 6.1% in
South Australia during 2024. Co-located battery storage was included in 41%
of new utility-scale project applications, up from 28% in 2023.

Interconnection queues in the United States held over 1,500 GW of proposed
solar and storage capacity at year end, with median wait times of 4.5 years.
